Applicability notes:
- 4.3 stop lamps: "Vehicles first used on or after 1 January 1971 must have 2 stop lamps, one on each side." "Additional stop lamps, over and above the mandatory requirements, must be tested." "However, if you are not sure if they are connected, you should give the benefit of the doubt." The section does not name a "high level" stop lamp; it is covered as an additional stop lamp.
- 4.4: "Vehicles first used before 1 April 1986 do not need to have hazard warning devices."
- 4.5: rear fog lamp required on vehicles "first used on or after 1 April 1980" (1 lamp, centre or offside).
- 4.6: reversing lamps inspected on vehicles "first used from 1 September 2009".
- 4.2: DRLs inspected if original equipment on vehicles "first used on or after 1 March 2018".
- 4.1.6: headlamp cleaning devices on vehicles first used on or after 1 September 2009, if fitted.
- 4.9.1: main beam tell-tale required on vehicles first used on or after 1 April 1986; "A hazard warning 'tell-tale' must be a flashing light."
- No general "lamp malfunction indicator" rule (a bulb-failure warning lamp counting as a defect) was found in section 4.

Petrol basic emissions test headline limits (8.2.1): "CO up to 0.2% at fast idle (2,500rpm to 3,000rpm)"; "HC up to 200ppm at fast idle (2,500rpm to 3,000rpm)"; "Lambda between 0.97 and 1.03 at fast idle (2,500rpm to 3,000rpm)"; "CO up to 0.3% at idle (450rpm to 1,500rpm)". Spark ignition vehicles first used on or after 1 August 1975 are inspected; manufacturer-specific limits apply where an exact match exists. Emission control equipment rejected if missing, modified or defective on vehicles first used on or after 1 September 2002 (as read, paraphrase).

Diesel default opacity limits (8.2.2.2, as read): first used before 1 July 2008: 2.5 m-1 non-turbo, 3.0 m-1 turbo; 1 July 2008 to 31 December 2013: 1.5 m-1; on or after 1 January 2014: 0.7 m-1. Plate value used where shown (0.05 added). DPF "clearly cut open and rewelded" rejected unless a valid reason such as cleaning is shown (paraphrase).

Fluid leaks (8.4.1): "You should fail a vehicle if a fluid leak creates a pool on the floor within 5 minutes that's more than 75mm in diameter". Coolant, screen wash and SCR fluid leaks are not failure reasons on their own (paraphrase). Class 3 not checked.

MOT rule changes 20 May 2018: major fault if the tester "can see smoke of any colour coming from the exhaust" or "finds evidence that the DPF has been tampered with". New items then included: "if tyres are obviously underinflated", "if the brake fluid has been contaminated", "for fluid leaks posing an environmental risk", "brake pad warning lights and if brake pads or discs are missing", "reversing lights on vehicles first used from 1 September 2009", "headlight washers on vehicles first used from 1 September 2009 (if they have them)", "daytime running lights on vehicles first used from 1 March 2018".

## Getting an MOT and MOT history

- First MOT due by "the third anniversary of its registration"; "An MOT lasts for a year." (https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot)
- Check MOT history shows pass or fail, mileage at each test, next due date, and for Great Britain tests "see what parts failed at each test, and if any parts had minor problems". Records: cars, motorcycles and vans since 2005; Northern Ireland since 2017. (https://www.gov.uk/check-mot-history)
- No separate "MOT: what's checked" page found; the nearest are "How the MOT test works" (https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot/the-mot-test) and "Car parts checked at an MOT" (link above).
